Sheet 01 · Method

Six stages, one leakage rule

Every stage below is fit on the training split first and only then applied to test. That single rule is what keeps the numbers on the spec plate above honest.

01

Load and inspect

2,930 rows across 82 columns from the Ames Housing dataset. First pass checks dtypes, cardinality, and the shape of SalePrice before touching missingness.

02

Structural missingness

Domain logic separates real gaps from encoded absence. Pool QC is blank because there is no pool, not because the value is unknown. Those are flagged before any statistical imputation runs.

03

Train / test split

2,344 training observations, 586 held out and untouched until final evaluation.

04

MICE-XGBoost imputation

Remaining Missing-at-Random gaps (e.g. Lot Frontage) are imputed with a fit/transform pattern. The imputer learns only from training data, then transforms test data, closing off leakage through the back door.

05

Tri-model training

Categoricals are one-hot encoded on train and aligned onto test. Target is log(SalePrice) to tame right skew. Linear Regression drops Total Bsmt SF, an exact sum of three other basement columns, to avoid perfect multicollinearity. The tree models keep the full feature set.

06

Explain with dalex

Explainers are built on the held-out test set, with predictions converted back from log-dollars to dollars. Three lenses: variable importance, partial dependence, and per-observation SHAP.

Sheet 04 · Behavior

Same feature, different stories

Each curve holds every other feature fixed and sweeps one variable, showing how a model's prediction moves in isolation.

Overall Qual, 1 to 10 rating
Field note Random Forest and XGBoost step up in discrete jumps as quality rating rises, roughly $50k to $250k across the range, because quality is really a small set of buckets, not a continuum. Linear Regression can only draw a straight line through that, so it stays nearly flat near the bottom of the chart and never captures the jump.
Gr Liv Area, above-ground living area in square feet
Field note Random Forest and XGBoost track each other closely, then plateau past roughly 2,500 square feet. There is little training data out there, so the trees stop extrapolating and flatten out. Linear Regression has no such brake: it keeps climbing indefinitely, exactly the kind of unchecked extrapolation behind the blown-up error on the scoreboard.

Sheet 06 · Findings

What the inspection actually earned

01

Missingness is not one thing

Structural gaps (no pool, no fireplace) were separated from genuine Missing-at-Random values before any statistical imputation ran. Treating them the same would have manufactured noise.

02

Leakage is a process discipline

MICE-XGBoost imputation was fit only on the training split and applied to test through fit/transform, the kind of detail that stays invisible in a metric until it is wrong in production.

03

Collinearity hits models differently

Total Bsmt SF is an exact sum of three other columns: fatal for Linear Regression's coefficients, a non-issue for tree-based splits.

04

Extrapolation is where models show themselves

Random Forest and XGBoost plateau in sparse regions of the feature space. Linear Regression keeps extrapolating in a straight line, visible proof of why its R2 goes negative.
